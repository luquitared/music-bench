Can AI hear music?

We asked audio models to turn a song into a full metadata timeline (chords, key, tempo, instruments, lyrics) and scored every answer against ground truth. The test is open: run it on your model and send a pull request.

The cliff: clean audio vs real recordings

On songs we generated from MIDI, the Gemini models get about 9 in 10 chords right. On real recordings the number falls, and on obscure songs it falls to around half. Qwen is the exception, and an odd one: weaker on the easy songs, best on the obscure ones (more below). Bars show the share of each song where the model names the correct chord at that moment (major/minor level, scored with mir_eval).

Generated songs can't be memorized and have perfect labels, so they are the easiest case. It is also likely the kind of data labs synthesize for training, which may be why the strongest models are near the ceiling there.

Listening, not reciting

Famous songs might be answered from memory: the chords to "Let It Be" are all over the internet. So two Beatles songs are pitch-shifted into new keys. A model reciting from memory reports the original key; a model that listens follows the shift.

Song by song

Chord accuracy per real song. Zweieck is an obscure German band; its songs change key and use denser harmony. The Beatles songs are simpler and cleaner, which helps as much as any familiarity.

Lyrics: words yes, timing so-so

Eight obscure Creative Commons songs with human-timed lyric lines. Bars show word accuracy (1 minus word error rate). Timing is the weak part: even the best model puts only about half of the lines within one second of the truth, and single runs sometimes shift the whole lyric timeline by 30 seconds or more.

What this means

  • Gemini 4 Neon leads overall, mainly on key tracking (92% on songs that change key) and lyrics, but it spends about 23k output and thinking tokens per song, roughly 7 times Argon.
  • Qwen 3.8 Omni Flash is the surprise. It is the cheapest model here and scores 71% on the obscure chords, where the best Gemini gets 48%, yet only 69% on the generated songs that every Gemini aces. It follows the pitch-shifted songs, so it is listening. But Isophonics, the source of the chord labels, is a standard training set for chord recognition, so prior exposure to these exact recordings can't be ruled out. It also failed 4 of its 40 real-song requests: its API rejects the longest song's upload, and it twice returned an empty answer.
  • Key, tempo and words are usable. Chords on unfamiliar recordings and lyric timestamps are not reliable from any model on its own; even the best puts only half of the lyric lines within a second.
  • For a production timeline, run dedicated tools for chords, beats and lyric alignment, and use a model to check and label their output.
  • Gemini 4 Argon refuses one Zweieck song on input every time (BlockedReason.OTHER, likely a copyright filter). It scores zero there, as every failed answer does.

Method

  • Generated set: 7 songs rendered from MIDI with a General MIDI soundfont, with key changes, 7th chords, instruments entering mid-song and spoken lyrics. Labels are exact.
  • Real chords and key: 6 Zweieck and 4 Beatles songs with human annotations from the Isophonics dataset, plus 2 pitch-shifted copies. Audio is matched to the annotated recordings by length. One Isophonics key label (Here Comes the Sun, listed as A minor) is corrected to A major.
  • Real lyrics: 8 English songs from JamendoLyrics with human line timings.
  • Prompt: one request per song asking for JSON with bpm, keys, chords, instruments and lyrics, each with start and end times. Default settings, 2 runs per song. A refused or unparseable answer scores zero for that song.
  • Caveats: 20 real songs is small; treat differences under about 5 points as noise. Instruments are only scored on the generated set, since none of the real datasets label them.
Code, data and submissions: github.com/luquitared/music-bench. Scores computed with mir_eval and jiwer. Datasets: Isophonics, JamendoLyrics. An Odapt project.
